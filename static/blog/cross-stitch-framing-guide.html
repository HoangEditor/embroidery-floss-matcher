How to Frame Cross Stitch: Lacing, Mounting & Professional Finishing

Published 2025 — Embroidery Floss Matcher Team

Framing is the final step that makes or breaks your project. Here's how to do it right.

Method 1: Lacing (Best)

The traditional, archival method. No adhesive touches the fabric. How: Center your piece on acid-free foam board. Fold excess fabric over the edges. Using strong thread, lace back and forth across the back — horizontal first, then vertical. Pull tight. The tension holds the fabric flat without glue. Time-consuming but museum-quality.

Method 2: Sticky Board (Easiest)

Peel-and-stick mounting board. Center your piece, press down. Instant results. Caution: The adhesive may yellow over decades. Not recommended for heirloom pieces. Perfect for gifts and seasonal decor.

Method 3: Pinning (Good Compromise)

Pin the fabric to acid-free foam board using stainless steel pins along the edges. More secure than sticky board, faster than lacing. Pins can rust over time — use stainless.

Mat or No Mat?

With mat: Professional look, keeps glass off the stitches (important for textured pieces with French knots or beads). Without mat: Cleaner for modern designs but glass touches stitches — use spacers.

Glass: Regular, Non-Glare, or UV?

UV-protective glass is worth it for pieces you'll display for decades. It prevents thread fading. For gifts, regular glass is fine.

Before framing, verify your thread colors are correct. Use our floss matcher if you substituted any colors — make sure they work together.
