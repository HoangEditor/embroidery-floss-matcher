How to Wash & Care for Embroidery: Preserve Your Hand-Stitched Work

Published 2025 — Embroidery Floss Matcher Team

You spent 200 hours stitching. Don't ruin it in the wash. Here's how to clean and preserve hand embroidery.

Before You Wash: The Colorfast Test

DMC and Anchor threads are colorfast — they shouldn't bleed. But reds and dark blues sometimes do. Test: Snip a small piece of each thread color, wet it, and press between white paper towels. Any color transfer = that thread bleeds. Pre-wash those skeins before stitching.

Washing Hand Embroidery

  1. Fill a clean sink with cool water + a drop of mild soap (baby shampoo or Orvus paste)
  2. Submerge the piece, gently swish — don't scrub
  3. If water colors, drain and repeat until clear
  4. Rinse with cool water until no soap remains
  5. Roll in a clean white towel to remove excess water — never wring
  6. Lay flat to dry, face-down on a clean towel

Ironing Embroidery

  • Iron face-down on a padded surface (towels on ironing board)
  • Use low-medium heat
  • Place a pressing cloth between iron and embroidery
  • Never iron metallic thread — it melts.
  • Satin/rayon thread: very low heat or no iron

Long-Term Storage

Store flat, not folded. Acid-free tissue paper between folds if necessary. Avoid direct sunlight — UV fades thread over years. Frame with UV-protective glass for display pieces.
