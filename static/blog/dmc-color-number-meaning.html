What Do DMC Thread Numbers Actually Mean? The Secret Numbering System

Published 2025 — Embroidery Floss Matcher Team

DMC numbers seem random — 310 is black, 666 is red, 3840 is blue. But there's a logic. Here's the hidden system.

The DMC Numbering Logic

DMC groups colors by hue families in numerical ranges. The first digit often indicates the color group:

  • 1xx: Yellows and golds
  • 2xx: Pinks and roses
  • 3xx: Browns, tans, reds (the 300s are the biggest range)
  • 4xx: Oranges and coppers
  • 5xx: Greens (yellow-greens)
  • 6xx: Purples and lavenders
  • 7xx: Blues
  • 8xx: Blue-greens and teals
  • 9xx: Dark greens and olives
  • 3000s: "Newer" browns and neutrals
  • 3600s: Pinks and mauves
  • 3800s: Deep reds and wines

Why Are Some Numbers Out of Order?

DMC's numbering dates back 100+ years. New colors got added wherever there was a gap, not sequentially. That's why you see 310 (black) next to 300 (mahogany) — 310 was added later. The system evolved, it wasn't designed from scratch.

The Letter Colors

B5200 (Bright white), Blanc (Natural white), and Ecru (Cream) are so old they predate the numbering system. They kept their original names.
